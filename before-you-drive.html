Before you drive

If you're not sure you're okay to drive, pause and use another way home. Feeling fine is not the same as being safe to drive.

Easy alternatives

If you're not sure, you don't have to drive.

  • Call someone you trust to drive you.
  • Book a taxi or rideshare.
  • Take public transit.
  • Wait until you're sure, or don't drive today.

What alcohol and drugs can do

  • Slow your reactions.
  • Affect your vision.
  • Make it harder to judge distance and speed.
  • Make you feel more sure of yourself than you should.

Some prescription and over-the-counter medicines can affect driving. Ask your doctor or pharmacist if you're unsure.

Source: NHTSA, Drug-Impaired Driving. Checked 12 November 2026. These are independent official pages. HomeCircle is not affiliated with them.

The figures

These figures are worded as their official sources word them. They were checked 12 November 2026.

Drunk-driving deaths, 2024

In 2024, 11,904 people died in drunk-driving crashes (BAC .08+), about 30% of US traffic deaths; about 32 a day, one every 44 minutes.

Period: 2024.

Source: NHTSA, Drunk Driving. Open the official page. Checked 12 November 2026.

Alcohol-related deaths at a BAC of .01 to .07, 2024

In 2024, 2,028 people were killed in alcohol-related crashes where a driver had a BAC of .01 to .07.

Even a small amount of alcohol can affect driving.

Period: 2024.

Source: NHTSA, Drunk Driving. Open the official page. Checked 12 November 2026.

Drugs detected at studied trauma centers, October–December 2020

56% of drivers in serious-injury and fatal crashes at studied trauma centers tested positive for at least one drug (Oct–Dec 2020).

This figure is for studied trauma centers in October–December 2020. It is not a national drug-impaired death count.

Period: October–December 2020, at studied trauma centers.

Source: NHTSA, Drug-Impaired Driving. Open the official page. Checked 12 November 2026.

Self-reported driving under the influence, 2023

In 2023, among people 16 and older, 15.5 million (5.9%) drove under the influence of alcohol, 12.1 million (4.6%) drove under the influence of cannabis, and 2.2 million (0.8%) drove under the influence of other illicit drugs in the past year.

Period: 2023, past year, people 16 and older.

Source: CDC, Impaired Driving Facts, citing the 2023 National Survey on Drug Use and Health (NSDUH). Page updated 28 January 2026. Open the official page. Checked 12 November 2026.

People often think they're fine after several drinks, but failing to recognize impairment is often a symptom of impairment.

It is illegal everywhere in the United States to drive impaired by alcohol, marijuana, opioids, meth, or any potentially impairing drug, prescribed or over-the-counter.

A national count of drug-impaired driving deaths is not known, because of data limitations.

Source: NHTSA, Drunk Driving and Drug-Impaired Driving; CDC, Impaired Driving Facts. Checked 12 November 2026. These are independent official pages. HomeCircle is not affiliated with them.

Legal limits

As a legal fact, the blood alcohol concentration (BAC) limit is .08 in most states, and .05 in Utah. HomeCircle doesn't restate these as advice. Check the official NHTSA Drunk Driving page.

Questions to ask yourself

If you're deciding whether to drive, you might ask yourself:

  • Do I feel fine, or am I actually fine to drive?
  • Is there another way home tonight?
  • If something happened on the drive, would it be worth having on my conscience?
  • Would I be happy for someone I care about to be in the car with me right now?
