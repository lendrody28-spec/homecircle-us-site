The 12 Steps

Many AA and NA members work through the 12 Steps over time, often with a sponsor. These are HomeCircle's own plain-language reflections on the idea behind each Step, not the official wording of AA or NA. Every group and member finds their own way through them. You can read the official Steps on AA's website (aa.org).

In HomeCircle's own plain-language words. These notes are HomeCircle's own summaries, not the official wording of AA or NA. For AA's own wording of the Twelve Steps, see aa.org.
Read the 12 Steps on AA's website (opens aa.org)Free recovery books

Step 1

Honesty

Getting honest that drinking or using has been calling the shots, and that willpower on its own hasn't fixed it. It's a starting point, not a judgment of your worth.

Where could a little more honesty make today lighter?

Step 2

Hope

Opening up to the idea that things can get better, with help from beyond yourself. That help can mean faith, the group, or simply people who've been there.

What gives you even a small bit of hope today?

Step 3

Letting help in

Choosing to stop carrying everything alone. People describe this in their own way: a faith, the fellowship, or the values they want to live by.

What is one thing you could stop carrying alone today?

Step 4

Taking stock

Writing an honest list of the patterns, fears and resentments that keep tripping you up, and your strengths as well. Many people do this with a sponsor so it stays fair and kind.

What is one strength you can name about yourself today?

Step 5

Telling someone

Sharing what you found with one person you trust. Saying it out loud often takes away some of its weight.

Who is one safe person you could be real with this week?

Step 6

Willingness

Becoming ready to let go of habits and reactions that hurt you or others. Willing is enough. Nobody expects perfect.

Which old reaction would you most like to let go of?

Step 7

Humility

Asking, in whatever way fits your beliefs, for help to change those patterns. It's a daily practice more than a one-off moment.

Where could asking for help make today easier?

Step 8

Facing the past

Writing down the people your drinking or using affected, and getting ready to put things right where you can.

Is there someone you'd like to make peace with, when the time is right?

Step 9

Making things right

Repairing harm directly where it's safe and won't cause new hurt. A sponsor or counselor can help you think through when and how.

What is one small way to be fair to someone today?

Step 10

Daily check-in

A regular look back at the day: what went well, where old patterns crept in, and owning mistakes early instead of letting them pile up.

What went well today, even if it was small?

Step 11

Quiet time

Making room for reflection, prayer or meditation, whatever suits you, to stay grounded and clear about what matters.

Could you find five quiet minutes for yourself today?

Step 12

Passing it on

Living these ideas day to day and offering a hand to people who are where you once were.

Who could you encourage today, even with a short message?

Where this comes from

The 12 Steps were first written down by Alcoholics Anonymous in the 1930s. Narcotics Anonymous and other fellowships adapted them. The notes on this page are HomeCircle's own plain-language summaries, not the official wording of AA or NA.
